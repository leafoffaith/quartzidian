From Pokémon to Telehealth, HCI has had one of the most interesting documented journeys that we are about to embark on. 

**The Birth And Emergence of HCI**

Over several decades, HCI has evolved and metamorphized into what it is now - enabling the confluence of man and machine. Although, we're far from a true convergence, there are now more natural and effective ways to interact with technology. Computers have become increasingly ubiquitous, and the focus is now on developing systems that can be widely used and adopted, regardless of the technical expertise of the user. 

In the 1980s, HCI took a turn with the development of GUIs, that replaced command-line interfaces and gave the general public hands on previously esoteric software and hardware. Soon after, the newly minted mouse, windows and icons all allowed us, the users, to interact intuitively with machines and let to the subsequent rise of 'personal computers'

The 90s saw the emergence of the 'World Wide Web', now a mainstream way of communication, that further bolstered the need for user-friendly interfaces. Slowly, web-based applications were being developed and web-design standards came about that focused on usability and accessibility. When mobile devices came in the 2000s, it became pertinent to create unique experiences for users that went above and beyond *windows* and *icons*. Designers were now tasked with the challenge of creating user-experiences for smaller screens and novel touch-based interactions. 

**HCI Now**

Today, HCI has become an interdisciplinary field drawing on a wide variety of domains and disciplines, primarily psychology, computer science and design. HCI researchers and practitioners are all on it to understand how people interact with technology and design interfaces that champion ease of use, effectiveness and accessibility. Social and cultural factors also influence a lot, when it comes down to the use of tech and also adopting it. Ethical implications of technology use are also taken into consideration. 

**An Example In Pokémon**

For fans of Pokémon, those who played Pokémon Soul Silver (Or Heart Gold) remember the Poke-walker, a round bespoke device that looked like a Pokéball with a cute monochrome interface. What make the Poke-walker so interesting was not the design of the device itself but that it was a genius tool that made people walk more. 
It was a simple pedometer in disguise that levelled up your Pokémon as you walked. The more you walked, the faster your Pokémon became stronger. 

This was over 10 years back when Fitbit was not even around and the concept of tracking steps was not even a fad. It was a stroke of brilliance by the Pokémon company to not just bring you closer to your 2D friends but to simply make you walk more. 

Several years later we would be introduced to the phenomena that took the world by storm - Pokémon Go. 

The use of wearable devices for tracking sleep, heart-rate and athletic performance is something that we're all very used to now. Many people have on their wrists, either a smart-watch or a fitness watch that tracks for them and keeps them in touch with their online world. This comes at the heels of people being too online.  

It has its good uses though, in football, players will wear GPS-tracking jerseys during games and training to analyze patterns in movement and performance on pitch, which would be impossible without the use of this technology. 

If you've seen Moneyball, you know how enticing data-driven efficiency is!

Another example is in basketball, where players wear sensor-equipped shirts that track their biometric data. This data is then used to monitor players' fatigue levels and reduce the risk of injury. 

There is absolutely no question in the efficacy of wearable technology. But we must not forget that it used to be much simpler and less accessible, at least for the general public. 

As HCI has transformed, it has had to mold itself to suit the needs of everyone. This also includes differently abled people, people with visual and/or motor impairments,  people with cognitive as well as sensory difficulties. 

Another concern that comes up is the design of interfaces, we need to make sure that they are as secure as they can be to protect the privacy of users. Recent scandals regarding the leak of personal information have shows the seriousness of the matter, of the personal information that is shared online. It is but essential now to keep in mind the security and privacy of users in mind while designing systems. 

**Cutting-edge HCI**

Technology has enabled us to interact with the world very differently. With VR, AR and XR, there are now worlds within worlds. New forms of interaction like voice-based interfaces, AR and VR(augmented and visual reality) These technologies have the power to create new user experiences and greatly enhance existing ones. 
Although these present new challenges for developers and designers to break through, but they also present new opportunities to innovate to push the human-race forward. 

Concluding, the history of HCI is the history of computing and technology, as it evolved from niche uses to it being in the hands of everyone. It has played an increasingly critical role in shaping accessible tech and computing, integrating tech in our day to day lives and pushing the boundaries of human-computer interactions. 

HCI is and always has been about people. It will continue to be designed with people in mind. HCI might be about humans interacting with screens of all shapes and sizes, but it is shaped by humans, for humans.
