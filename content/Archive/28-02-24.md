## When was the last time you were sick?
Shaurya last felt sick, annoyed and dozy not too long ago. It was last week, or maybe the week before that. The cool spring breeze and the warmth of the summer sun were enough reasons for Shaurya to abandon his jackets, of which he has many and don his kurtas for which he known. Summertime is the time of the year when Shaurya feels most happy. The memories, the feelings and the sensation of being free. The summers he spent in Kolkata. Where he used to sit with his mum and 'Gigin' on the balcony and practice handwriting. Shaurya's handwriting isn't pretty anymore but it was once his Kindergarten teacher's favourite thing. Shaurya remembers the day his teacher asked him after he moved out of junior school, "Is your handwriting still good?"

It wasn't and still isn't. Shaurya hasn't found stillness since he left being a kid in junior school. His handwriting is a direct projection of his mind that is full of thoughts, much of them useless, but full of them still. To put that to paper is a task. Yet, Shaurya loves to write. Even though Shaurya has invested so much in fancy keyboards and typing instruments, he hasn't forgotten the feel of a writing instrument on paper. The light scratching against an 80gsm paper leaves a mark, more figurative than literal. 

Shaurya wishes that he had more patience. Patience enough to sit down and journal. Yes, stream-of-consciousness typing also requires patience of some form, but writing demands a lot more concentration. Or maybe typing comes easier.

Shaurya's memories however pretty are suddenly overwhelmed by anxiety. The tightness in his chest. The one he feels right now at the thought of talking to Mansi. Wonder what he fears most? He doesn't know what she has to say. He is scared that he has done something wrong or been wily. Trying to separate the two. He feels guilty maybe. He feels guilty that he has indeed preferred one over the other even though Mansi is the one who introduced them. 

Who is he angry at? Mansi or himself? He is angry and a little frustrated at both. Mansi has not been forthcoming about the things that have been bothering her. Which is not a good space to be in. Considering how cold she got suddenly. In return, she has been a little vindictive he feels. 

In going to meet Chuck alone. Maybe that's what bothers Shaurya and pushes him to act out even more. That behaviour alone irritates Shaurya and that's why he does not wish to be friends with Mansi. *Is there prejudice because she is a Sindhi and Makhija was too? It is unfair but Shaurya might feel some inhibitions. It may be a generalisation but what if you never outgrow your people? *She drinks in the morning. Smokes up. These are not attractive traits. The first meeting they had was also when she was drunk out of her mind. Is that attractive? There might be a million reasons Shaurya can come up with but the fact of the matter is, some people just might never get along. You stick with the people who do get along with you.

The anxiety-inducing part is what she might accuse Shaurya of. Shaurya did say that he only relates to her through his social mask, his pseudo-identity and that might have undermined her struggles, which is fair. She might feel angry about that but that still means that she should talk about it. It is also true that Shaurya feels that there is an invisible wall that he cannot overcome which might be because `we` are fundamentally different. Or the fact that she did in fact make things harder for him with the initial back and forth. *Shaurya has some regrets there that are more for him to work through than her and he probably should not expect anything from her.*

Shaurya also feels a little jealous of the relationship between M and C because yes, they are closer and yes C will choose M. Shaurya may not be able to support C emotionally, which is what he is afraid of and will slowly get fazed out. It is natural and if it happens, it happens. He does feel that there is a chance and a good one at that for his friendship with C to persist because it is very easy for him to hang out with her and vice versa. So, he just needs to make sure that his interactions with M are reduced to a minimum or null if possible. It is best to let these things end than fester. 

What's the point in putting in energy which will never be returned to you? It's like sending your CV to a bunch of companies that will never respond to you. Yes, there might be a way to salvage it by being vulnerable yourself but Shaurya already has been pretty vulnerable and he falls into this trend where he lets out too much instead of withholding some of it. Which is required. Withholding is required to protect ourselves and to not give that power over to people. It is to own your story. That does not mean you close off but that you understand where the boundary lies.

Shaurya wishes to get done with this conversation as soon as possible as he does not feel good about the idea of it happening at all. However, it is not proper to cut off people whom you had deemed important in any capacity for any amount of time. 

Shaurya does not desire a particular outcome but wants a resolution. Either M realises that the way forward is to gradually open up and try OR let it die on the vine. Shaurya hopes it leads one way or another. Either way, there is no regret. There is no problem. Shaurya has found friends. Shaurya has friends. Shaurya has never been truly alone at any point in life. Even in family and that is because Shaurya loves the people he loves. Not today but maybe tomorrow, there will come a person who can truly make him feel the things he wants to feel. 

In the meantime, it also means that Shaurya loves himself and is warm to these parts of him that are insecure. To make sure that they are validated. To understand where it might be coming from. *Shaurya also feels hurt and sad by not being able to talk to Trevor anymore.* 

Perhaps that is another thing that has been bringing him down. So, there is a lot going on. No one will truly understand what you are going through. You have to understand that you are your only companion, you have to have dominance and control over your thoughts and emotions and not let them overpower you. Others can't and should not be expected to help you

Finally, Shaurya is glad that he has been trying to get these things out of him. Shaurya holds things to himself too much. This isn't good for his wellbeing. Shaurya needs to be open and upfront with everything. Only if he is true to himself will he be able to deal with others. Honesty, rather radical honesty is essential for a life well lived.
